# Meetings (Grove)

Grove calls meetings "the medium of managerial work". They're where information gathering, giving, and decisions actually happen. The goal is not fewer meetings, but meetings that each have a job. He splits them into two kinds.

## Process-oriented meetings (regular, scheduled)

Purpose: knowledge sharing and information exchange on a predictable rhythm, so issues surface before they become crises. Because they're regular, people save up non-urgent items for them instead of interrupting all week.

### The 1:1

Grove's highest-leverage regular meeting: a short investment that shapes a person's work for weeks.

- **It's the subordinate's meeting.** They set the agenda and bring an outline. The manager's role is to learn and coach, not to deliver a status lecture.
- **Frequency follows TRM**, not seniority or habit. Low TRM on current work → weekly. High TRM → every few weeks. Reassess when their assignment changes.
- **Length:** Grove argued for at least an hour, so there's room to get past status to the real issue. Many teams now run 30–45 minutes weekly. Recommend whatever leaves room for the second and third layers of a topic, and say that the hour is Grove's number.
- **Location:** Grove suggested holding it in the subordinate's space, which gives them home-field comfort and keeps their materials at hand. Remote equivalent: their doc, their screen.
- **Ask one more question.** When the report seems done, ask another. Grove's point is that the important issue often comes last.
- **Write it down.** Both sides keep notes on commitments, and the next 1:1 starts with follow-ups.

**Agenda skeleton** (report-owned):

1. Their topics: blockers, decisions they need, things they're unsure about
2. Indicators and progress on their work (see `indicators.md`)
3. Problems on the horizon, raised before they're urgent
4. Development: what they're learning, what they want next
5. Manager's context: only what changes their work

### Staff meeting

The manager plus their direct reports.

- **Purpose:** peer-level information exchange and problems that span more than one report, where peers resolve issues with each other rather than routing everything through the manager.
- **Manager as moderator, not star.** Keep it on topic and draw out quiet people; don't make it a broadcast.
- **What doesn't belong:** status that could be read async, or one-on-one issues that only involve two people.
- Mix a fixed portion (indicators review) with an open portion (topics people bring).

### Operations review

A periodic (monthly or quarterly) forum where people who don't usually work together, such as a team and its skip-level, review results.

- **Format:** presenters, the manager of the presenters, a senior manager, and peers who learn from the exchange.
- **Teaching value:** juniors see how seniors probe, and seniors hear ground truth.
- **Role guidance:**
  - **Presenter:** concise, honest about problems, with data.
  - **Supervisor:** asks the questions that teach.
  - **Senior manager:** probes, asks "why", and spots patterns across teams.

## Mission-oriented meetings (ad hoc)

Called to produce a specific output, usually a decision, and then disband.

- **The chair must know the objective.** Before the meeting, ask what decision or output it should produce, and whether a meeting is actually needed to get it.
- **Right people only.** Grove's guideline is that past 6–8 people, a meeting becomes a show rather than a working session.
- **Prepare and pre-read.** Put the options and data in front of people beforehand.
- **Close with minutes:** the decision, the owner, the next steps, and anything still open. Send them promptly so people who weren't there know what happened.
- **If no decision or output came out, the meeting failed.** Note what was missing (information, a decider, a key person) and fix that before rescheduling.

## Diagnosing a bloated calendar

When a manager says they're "in meetings all day":

1. **Classify each recurring meeting** as process or mission. Mission meetings that keep recurring are often missing a decision owner.
2. **For each one, ask Grove's leverage questions:** Does it raise team output? Would it happen without me? Am I there to gather information, give information, or decide? If none of the three, drop it or send a delegate.
3. **Collapse status meetings into indicators.** Status should be read, not recited.
4. **Rebalance 1:1 frequency by TRM** instead of giving everyone the same cadence.
5. **Batch.** Cluster 1:1s and protect contiguous blocks of focus time.
